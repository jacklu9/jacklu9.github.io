Decision authority in automated booking

A comparison of two approval processes that produce the same reservation.

In both records, a community hall is reserved for 7:30 on Thursday. Both records show a coordinator’s name in the approval field. In both, she approved the time before the booking closed.

An editable recommendation

The booking assistant proposes 7:30 and displays the opening hours, an earlier opening, and a note the coordinator can edit before giving her approval. She checks that the last bus leaves well after the event and elects the later time. If the earlier opening had suited the group better, she could have selected it without losing the booking.

A recommendation with costly review

On a parallel screen, the booking assistant proposes 7:30. The other openings are hidden by default. A small link offers manual review, but the notice beside it says the hold will expire before a manual review can be completed. The coordinator presses Approve because the group still needs a room. She never sees the earlier opening. The log fills in exactly the same two fields.

The screen with the deadline leaves the coordinator accountable for a choice it made difficult to refuse. If the coordinator is going to answer for the booking later, it matters whether she could see alternatives, weigh them in time, and choose one without an unreasonable penalty. A checkbox can't recover those possibilities afterward. Neither can asking whether the recommendation happened to be correct.

Change the booking to a public allocation and the same missing context becomes harder to ignore. A short record may be enough to tell people where to go next Thursday; it may not be enough to tell them which parts of the process they can challenge.
