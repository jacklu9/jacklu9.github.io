Why Wavevectors Fold Back

Use translation symmetry to put wavevectors in one zone, then distinguish a folded free-electron crossing from a true gap.

A line of equally spaced atoms turns a wavevector problem into a phase problem. The lattice can compare the phase of a wave before and after one repeat distance. Two waves that turn through different numbers of full cycles between equivalent sites can still make the same phase change from one cell to the next. This is why the horizontal axis of a band diagram can be restricted to a single neighborhood of reciprocal space. It is not a limit on the wavelengths an electron’s wavefunction can contain.

From wavelength to translation phase

For a free plane wave eiKxe^{iKx}, one cycle requires Kλ=2πK\lambda=2\pi for K>0K>0. The de Broglie relation p=h/λp=h/\lambda becomes p=ℏKp=\hbar K in one dimension when the sign of KK is included. Combining this with E=p2/(2m)E=p^2/(2m) gives a parabola, E=ℏ2K2/(2m)E=\hbar^2K^2/(2m). In a crystal the electron feels a periodic potential. The free parabola is then a starting model, not the complete dispersion.

Let V(x+a)=V(x)V(x+a)=V(x). Translation by one period commutes with the single-electron Hamiltonian, so its energy states can be chosen to have a definite translation phase. Write that phase as eikae^{ika}. Dividing the corresponding state by eikxe^{ikx} removes the change over a translation, leaving a periodic function. This is the one-dimensional Bloch form.

ψk(x+a)=eikaψk(x),uk(x)=e−ikxψk(x),uk(x+a)=uk(x).\psi_k(x+a)=e^{ika}\psi_k(x),\qquad u_k(x)=e^{-ikx}\psi_k(x),\qquad u_k(x+a)=u_k(x).

The Bloch wavevector kk labels the phase under translation and ℏk\hbar k is called crystal momentum. In general it is not the mechanical momentum eigenvalue of the electron. The periodic part uku_k can contain several Fourier harmonics, so the full wavefunction can contain plane waves with several different momenta.

One cell of reciprocal space is enough for the label

Adding G=2π/aG=2\pi/a to kk adds a full cycle to the phase across one cell: ei(k+G)a=eikae^{i(k+G)a}=e^{ika}. For a translation by nana the extra factor is also one. All labels k+nGk+nG for integer nn describe the same translation phase. In three dimensions, reciprocal-lattice vectors are the vectors G\mathbf G for which eiG⋅R=1e^{i\mathbf G\cdot\mathbf R}=1 for every lattice translation R\mathbf R.

For a unique representative of the one-dimensional phase, choose the zone centered on 0 and count just one copy of its equivalent endpoints. The boundaries are halfway between reciprocal-lattice points 0 and ±G\pm G. The corresponding three-dimensional construction gives all points at least as close to the reciprocal origin as to any other reciprocal-lattice point.

−πa≤k<πa,K=k+n2πa.-\frac{\pi}{a}\leq k<\frac{\pi}{a},\qquad K=k+n\frac{2\pi}{a}.

Slide an extended wavevector past π/a\pi/a. Its reduced representative wraps to the other side of the zone, but the phase measured from one site to the next changes continuously. Plane waves with these two wavevectors coincide on the lattice sites; between the sites they need not. A matching phase does not make them the same free-electron state.

For example, with a=0.40a=0.40 nm, G≈15.7 nm−1G\approx15.7\,\mathrm{nm}^{-1}. A free plane wave at K=1.30π/aK=1.30\pi/a reduces to k=−0.70π/ak=-0.70\pi/a, or about −5.50 nm−1-5.50\,\mathrm{nm}^{-1}. The free plane waves at KK and kk have different kinetic energies. Folding onto the same horizontal coordinate keeps both energies, so the reduced-zone diagram needs multiple branches, not a single relabeled parabola.

Same phase does not mean the same band

A full Bloch state can be rewritten with the label k+Gk+G by replacing its periodic factor with e−iGxuk(x)e^{-iGx}u_k(x). The product is unchanged at every point, not just on the lattice sites. This is a change of representation. Substituting ei(k+G)xe^{i(k+G)x} for eikxe^{ikx} while keeping the periodic factor unchanged is a different operation: in the free case it gives a different plane wave. A band diagram uses labels such as E1(k)E_1(k) and E2(k)E_2(k) because the translation phase is not enough to specify the energy or reconstruct the state.

The horizontal line between the zone edges is also not a continuum of independent states in a finite model. For a ring of NN periods with periodic boundary conditions, eikNa=1e^{ikNa}=1, so the permitted kk values are spaced by 2π/(Na)2\pi/(Na). Exactly NN distinct values fit in a half-open zone of width 2π/a2\pi/a. Enlarging the ring makes the labels denser without changing the zone width. This is one way to see why the number of states per band scales with the number of unit cells.

A periodic potential can open a gap at a folded crossing

At the right zone boundary k=G/2k=G/2, the free plane waves at kk and k−Gk-G have opposite wavevectors and equal energy E0E_0. Without a periodic potential the two folded branches meet. The change of plot coordinates has not removed any allowed energy.

Now write the weak periodic potential as Fourier components VGeiGxV_G e^{iGx}. The component at GG connects the two plane waves because it multiplies ei(k−G)xe^{i(k-G)x} into eikxe^{ikx}. For a real potential the coefficient at −G-G is the complex conjugate of VGV_G. If other waves are far away in energy, keep just the two equal-energy waves. In that basis, and allowing a common average potential V0V_0, the boundary Hamiltonian and its eigenvalues are

H=(E0+V0VGVG∗E0+V0),E±=E0+V0±∣VG∣.H=\begin{pmatrix}E_0+V_0&V_G\\ V_G^*&E_0+V_0\end{pmatrix},\qquad E_{\pm}=E_0+V_0\pm|V_G|.

The determinant condition is (E0+V0−E)2−∣VG∣2=0(E_0+V_0-E)^2-|V_G|^2=0, so the splitting is 2∣VG∣2|V_G|. The new states combine oppositely directed waves to make standing-wave patterns. Their densities preferentially sample different parts of the periodic potential. It is this coupling, not the bookkeeping convention that puts two branches on the same graph, that changes the energies.

For a=0.40a=0.40 nm and V(x)=0.12cos⁡(2πx/a)V(x)=0.12\cos(2\pi x/a) eV, the Fourier coefficients at ±G\pm G are each 0.06 eV. The weak-potential model predicts a 0.12 eV boundary gap. Using ℏ2/(2me)≈0.0381 eV nm2\hbar^2/(2m_e)\approx0.0381\,\mathrm{eV\,nm^2}, the free crossing is at 0.0381(π/0.40)2≈2.350.0381(\pi/0.40)^2\approx2.35 eV. The two levels are about 2.29 and 2.41 eV. If the cosine amplitude goes to zero, the levels meet again, but the folding is still defined.

From a local gap to a material

This calculation is local to one crossing and assumes weak coupling to other waves. It does not place the conduction minimum or establish the fundamental band gap of a real three-dimensional solid. Other directions and bands can offer states at the same energies. A band calculation can give several different dispersions with the same lattice translations. Once the dispersion is known, count the states and their occupations or use its slope and curvature to understand motion.

States split at the boundary, but another band has an allowed state between the two levels at a different kk. Is that splitting a global forbidden interval?

No. A gap at one wavevector is a local separation. A global forbidden interval may not contain an allowed state at any wavevector.
