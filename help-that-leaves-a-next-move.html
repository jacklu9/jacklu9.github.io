Help That Leaves a Next Move

A correct worksheet can conceal the moment a learner stopped making decisions.

A child sees an equal sign and a blank on opposite sides of a sum. Entering the number an adult supplies finishes the exercise. On the next sheet the blank has moved. Now the child has to decide what the equal sign requires, not recall where the adult put the answer.

Rittle-Johnson (2006) studied 85 children in grades three through five working on mathematical equivalence. Those who explained correct solutions transferred the procedure more successfully to changed problems, including at a two-week delay. Direct instruction helped students acquire and retain a correct procedure but did not outperform invention on that study’s transfer measures. Finishing the exercise and recognizing the occasion for using the same idea are different accomplishments.

The difference appeared on a larger set of classroom exercises in Bastani et al. (2025). Two forms of GPT-4 assistance improved students’ practice scores at one Turkish high school. On the later exam without their practice resources, students who had used general chat scored about 17% below the control group’s mean. The study did not detect the same drop with an alternative tutor that gave guidance and had teacher-informed material available. It changed both what the tutor knew and how it answered. The comparison is not a reason to deprive someone of an explanation when they need it. It is a reason to check who makes the next relevant move.

That question also matters for an adult reviewing a recommendation. In a meal-choice study, participants were less likely to agree with a model’s mistaken choice of ingredient to remove under a group of designs that delayed the recommendation, made it available on request, or first required an independent choice, compared with designs where the explanation appeared immediately (Buçinca et al., 2021). Delaying a recommendation and making a learner attempt a step are not identical interventions. Each changes when someone can form a judgment of their own. That is one part of finding evidence that could reverse a decision. A good follow-up leaves the person with a fresh decision and checks what they recognize before anyone tells them where to look.
