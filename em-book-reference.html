Back at the bench

The plates are still separated. We have not filled the gap with an unseen wire. What has changed is what we can say about the space between them.

The meter and the probe answer different questions. The meter records free charge crossing a section of wire per unit time. The probe measures a field at its own position. During charging, charge accumulates on the plates and the field in the gap changes. Once an ideal capacitor reaches a steady voltage, its conduction current can vanish while its electric field remains. A nonzero electric field does not require a local flow of free charge.

That distinction also explains why a charge-free part of the gap can contain a field. Gauss’s law says that its net outward displacement flux is zero; it does not say that every field arrow there is zero. A field can enter the region through one side and leave through another. The closed surface measures their balance. Its shape alone does not determine the field at each point: we still need the sources, material properties, and geometry.

The apparent break in the magnetic account is resolved by the changing field. One surface spanning a loop cuts the wire; another passes through the gap. During charging, conduction current contributes on the first, while changing electric displacement contributes on the second. Ampère– Maxwell’s law gives the same circulation for that one loop. Charge has not jumped across the insulator. The field-change term belongs in the magnetic law alongside the transport of charge.

When the drive repeats sinusoidally, phasors let us keep the amplitude and phase at every point without rewriting the common oscillation each time. Restoring the time factor and taking the real part gives the real field again. A rotating arrow on a complex plane describes that calculation; it does not by itself describe a rotating physical field. Likewise, the direction in which a phase pattern advances and the direction in which a local electric field points are separate pieces of information.

The two-layer example brings the questions together. With no initial interface charge, permittivities determine the initial field division. If the initial field division gives unequal conduction currents in the two layers, charge accumulates at their interface. Gauss’s jump condition then requires the fields to adjust. In the final steady state the conduction currents match, even though the field strengths usually do not. Throughout the transient, the sum of conduction and displacement current is continuous. Each earlier definition now has a job in one connected explanation.

We can now read a field picture more carefully: distinguish a value at a point from variation around it, a flux balance from a circulation, and a spatial direction from a time trace. The next question is what forms those fields can take as they propagate through space. That will require solving Maxwell’s equations with suitable material and boundary data. The wave chapters can begin from here, with the geometry and the meaning of the symbols already in place.

Symbols and definitions at a glance

The same printed letter can have different meanings in different examples. A definition in the local problem takes priority. In particular, V may mean a volume, a potential, or a voltage amplitude; T may be a period, while upright T is the unit tesla. A subscript usually identifies a component, a region, or a limiting value. It does not automatically mean a derivative.

The assumptions behind a simple material equation

Linear: doubling the input field doubles the material response. Isotropic: no spatial direction has a different response. Homogeneous: material properties do not vary with position. Time-independent: those properties do not change with time. Instantaneous: the response has no memory in the time-domain model. These are separate statements. They must not be inferred merely because a formula is written compactly.

Questions that test the picture

These checks concern the meaning of the equations, rather than speed of calculation. Try to picture the stated region, loop, or measurement before reading each explanation.

A uniform field crosses a closed box. Does zero net flux mean nothing crosses? No. The field enters one side and leaves the other. Each face can have a nonzero contribution, but their signed sum is zero. Divergence measures the imbalance, not the total amount crossing in either direction.

Field lines bend. Must the curl be nonzero? No. Curl compares circulation around an infinitesimal loop with its area. The field strength on neighbouring paths matters as well as the direction. Circular field lines with magnitude proportional to 1/r have zero curl away from their singular axis, whereas rigid rotation has nonzero curl.

A Gaussian sphere encloses a complicated source. Is the field constant on it? The choice of surface does not change the physical field. Constant magnitude on that sphere requires symmetry of the entire source and boundary configuration. Gauss’s law fixes the total flux even when the local values are unknown.

A capacitor gap carries no conduction current. Why can it still affect magnetic

circulation? Charging changes D in the gap. Ampère–Maxwell’s law contains both J and ∂tD. They ensure that two surfaces sharing one loop give the same circulation even when one cuts a wire and another passes between the plates.

A complex phasor rotates after the time factor is restored. Is the physical electric

field rotating? That rotation occurs in the auxiliary complex plane. A real field with only one spatial component oscillates along one physical line. To determine physical vector rotation, reconstruct all spatial components and compare their time dependence.

Two materials have continuous tangential electric fields. Are the full fields equal?

Only their two tangential components must agree under the ordinary interface assumptions. Their normal components depend on permittivity and free surface charge. A vector contains more information than one of its projections.

Conduction currents differ on opposite sides of an interface. Is charge conservation

violated? The difference can accumulate as surface charge or be redistributed by a sheet current. The correct balance includes ∂tρs and ∇s · Js. In the two-layer transient, conduction-current imbalance is exactly balanced by the displacement-current difference.

What completes a field problem? Maxwell’s equations constrain fields, but a particular physical solution also needs its sources, material relations, geometry, and appropriate initial and boundary data. A formula is useful only when these assumptions, its units, and the meaning of its components remain visible.
