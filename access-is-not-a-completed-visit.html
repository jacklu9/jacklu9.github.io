Access Is Not a Completed Visit

The availability of a route tells little about who could actually use it when it mattered.

A resident walks to a collection point that appears on the neighborhood water map. The point is real, and it normally provides an improved source. Today it is closed. A survey that asks whether the household has an improved source can correctly record yes. A service record that treats that answer as proof of water obtained on this trip has answered a different question.

A survey across nineteen Rohingya camps in Cox’s Bazar found near-universal reported access to improved water sources, while an estimated 44% of surveyed households said water from their usual sources had been continuously available in the preceding week (Butt et al., 2025). There is a second gap after the tap opens. In a western Kenyan spring-protection study, contamination fell much more at the springs than in water tested at home, and some households shifted visits from other sources to a protected spring (Kremer et al., 2011). Location, continuity, and what reaches someone’s home do not move together automatically.

Sometimes low recorded use reflects a choice rather than exclusion. In interviews following augmentative and alternative communication provision, one participant used new equipment for remote activities while continuing to speak naturally with her partner at home. Another stopped using the new device after integration difficulties and continued with existing aided communication (Broomfield et al., 2024). A log that labels the unused device as a failure in both homes would miss two different stories. A log that celebrates equipment delivery in both would do the same.

The relevant question depends on the occasion. On a water collection day, ask which households got water, which tried and could not, and which found another source. With a communication aid, ask which activities the person wants to join and what alternatives are available on the occasions they choose not to use it. More recorded use can signal a welcome new option, or the disappearance of an old one. The cases missing from a service count can tell the institutions something important, but only if a person who chose another option is not treated as equivalent to someone who tried and could not proceed.
