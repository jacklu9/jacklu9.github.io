1.2.4 Time-varying fields and conservation of charge

1.2.5 Faraday’s law

Charge balance explains how sources can change. It has not yet told us how a changing magnetic field constrains the electric field around it. This is where the loop viewpoint earns its place: the relevant electric measurement is circulation, rather than outward flux.

Magnetic flux and electric circulation are different geometrical measurements. Choose a fixed closed curve C and an open surface S whose edge is that curve. Magnetic flux is measured through

Electric circulation is measured around C:

The symbol EC denotes electromotive force, or emf. Despite its name, emf is not a force in newtons: its units are volts. For a stationary loop it is the line integral of electric force per unit positive charge, evaluated everywhere at one common instant. Its work-per-charge interpretation uses those instantaneous field values; it need not equal the work on a real charge taking finite time around a changing field. The curve can be imaginary; a wire is not needed for an electric field to have circulation.

Increasing positive Bz therefore corresponds to negative azimuthal electric field. The electric field circles the flux region; it does not point along B. The reduction to 2πrEϕ uses the stated symmetry, not Faraday’s law alone.

If the chosen loop is a closed conducting circuit, this electric field can drive a current. The current’s magnetic response opposes the change in linked flux; this is Lenz’s law. The opposition is to the change in flux, so a decreasing upward flux would give the opposite current direction. In a general geometry, the circulation law fixes an integral, and does not by itself specify the field on each part of the loop.

The curl vector specifies the axis and strength of local electric circulation. A time-varying B is associated with this circulating part of E; the equation does not say that E is parallel or directly proportional to ∂tB.

In electrostatics, E = −∇V and its circulation around any closed loop is zero. An electric field with nonzero curl cannot be represented by −∇V alone. An induced field may still coexist with an electrostatic contribution; Faraday’s law constrains the curl, not every part of the electric field.
