A charging capacitor exposes the missing term. Return to the gap at the beginning of the chapter. We can now make its puzzle precise: one magnetic circulation must have one value, however we choose to span its loop. Take the wire delivering current I(t) to the positive plate of the capacitor. Draw a loop C around that wire. A flat surface S1 spanning C cuts the wire, so conduction current passes through it. The same loop also bounds a bulged surface S2 that bends around the plate and passes through the insulating gap. No free carriers cross the gap, so conduction current through S2 is zero in the ideal model.

The circulation on the left depends only on the same loop C. It cannot equal I for one spanning surface and zero for another. The steady-current version has therefore omitted something essential when charge is accumulating.
