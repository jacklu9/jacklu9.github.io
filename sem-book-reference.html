Symbol reference

The symbols below collect the meanings introduced along the way. A reused letter is identified by its physical context and units.

SymbolMeaning
E, Ec, EvElectron energy, conduction-band edge, valence-band edge.
Eg = Ec − EvFundamental band gap.
k, kWavevector and a scalar wavevector coordinate or magnitude, as specified locally; not position.
kBBoltzmann constant; distinguished from wave number k.
Free-electron mass and effective mass in a band.
g(E), f(E)Available states per volume per energy; probability of electron occupation.
EF , EiFermi level; intrinsic Fermi level at the same temperature.
n, p, niElectron, hole and intrinsic carrier concentrations.
ND, NADonor and acceptor concentrations.
e > 0Elementary charge; electron charge −e, hole charge +e.
V , EElectrostatic potential and electric field: E = −dV /dx.
µ, τ, DMobility, momentum-relaxation time and diffusion coefficient.
J, IConventional current density and current, with I = JA for uniform J.
ρch, ρCharge density and electrical resistivity, respectively.

In the state-counting derivation, V denotes sample volume; in electrostatics it denotes potential, with the units making the distinction explicit.

The function exp(u) = eu uses the mathematical number e ≃ 2.718, whereas a charge factor such as en uses the elementary charge. The argument u of an exponential, logarithm or trigonometric function must be dimensionless.

The symbol p also denotes momentum in the wave–particle formulas; elsewhere it denotes hole concentration. Its meaning is fixed by the formula and units.

At 300 K, the rounded thermal energy used in the worked problems is kBT ≃ 0.026 eV = 26 meV; the thermal voltage is kBT/e ≃ 0.026 V = 26 mV.

Formula sheet and reading checks

Wavevector and band curvature

Available states and occupation

The DOS expressions are per unit volume for three-dimensional parabolic bands, including spin. f is dimensionless; gf is a concentration per energy.

Nondegenerate equilibrium populations

Replacing n by ND or p by NA needs complete ionisation, majority-carrier dominance and negligible compensation.

Transport and electrostatics

Before calculating, identify three things.

1. Is the horizontal axis x or k? 2. Is the quantity a state count, an occupation probability, or a carrier concentration? 3. Is the arrow a particle-motion direction or a conventional-current direction? 波矢不等于位置；电子运动方向不等于电流方向。

Unit conversions: 1 cm−3 = 106 m−3; 1 cm2/(V s) = 10−4 m2/(V s); 1 µm2 = 10−12 m2.
