Remove the field, but make one side more crowded
Return to a crossing plane inside the sample. This time set the electric field to zero, but suppose
the region on its right contains more electrons than the region on its left. Keep the random velocity
distributions the same in shape on both sides. Before writing an equation, decide which side sends
more particles across the plane.
Both sides send a fraction of their population across. The crowded side sends more, so the net
electron transfer is leftward, toward lower density. No electron has to sense which side is crowded:
unequal populations already produce unequal crossing counts. This spreading is diffusion. Unless
something maintains the imbalance, the density profile evolves as it spreads.

The two changes to our sample now fit in one expression. A uniform concentration leaves only
drift; a zero field leaves only diffusion. When both are present, evaluate their signs separately. A
zero meter reading might mean both terms vanish, as in the original uniform sample, or it might
mean two nonzero terms cancel. A spatial band diagram will let us distinguish those situations.
