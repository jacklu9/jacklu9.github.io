1.2.2 Total current expression

Remove the field, but make one side more crowded

Return to a crossing plane inside the sample. This time set the electric field to zero, but suppose the region on its right contains more electrons than the region on its left. Keep the random velocity distributions the same in shape on both sides. Before writing an equation, decide which side sends more particles across the plane.

Both sides send a fraction of their population across. The crowded side sends more, so the net electron transfer is leftward, toward lower density. No electron has to sense which side is crowded: unequal populations already produce unequal crossing counts. This spreading is diffusion. Unless something maintains the imbalance, the density profile evolves as it spreads.

If holes were more numerous on the right instead, they would also spread left, but their positive charge would make their current point left too.

Make the densities equal again while leaving the random speeds unchanged. The crossing rates balance and diffusion vanishes. That returns us to the first sample: motion alone was never the missing ingredient; the missing ingredient was an imbalance.

Let the field and the crowding act together

Keep the density increasing to the right and restore an electric field. A rightward field makes electrons drift left, reinforcing their leftward diffusion. The two changes to our sample now fit in one expression. A uniform concentration leaves only drift; a zero field leaves only diffusion. When both are present, evaluate their signs separately. A zero meter reading might mean both terms vanish, as in the original uniform sample, or it might mean two nonzero terms cancel. A spatial band diagram will let us distinguish those situations.
