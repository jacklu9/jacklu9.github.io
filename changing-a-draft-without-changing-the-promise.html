A Better Sentence, a Different Promise

A clean revision can take away the sender’s right to decide what they are agreeing to.

A volunteer tells an organizer, “The room should be free; I’m checking now.” An editing tool proposes a brisker reply: “The room is free.” The organizer prints an announcement, and the volunteer discovers that the venue cannot be used. The edit changed the world of the message: what remained to be checked became something another person could rely on.

A mismatch this large can be invisible to both speakers. Robertson and Díaz (2022) observed role-play conversations mediated by translation. In one, a parent asked a prospective caretaker about a child’s cashew allergy and an epinephrine autoinjector. The caretaker read the translation as a question about a stubborn child and reassured the parent. They continued with different understandings until the later review revealed the mismatch. Finding a way to say the problem aloud is part of repairing a conversation without replacing it. A more fluent answer would not have repaired the missing referent. The two participants needed to discover what the other meant while their next decision was still open.

Authorship can also be made visible in the work of editing. Jamie Preece and colleagues (2024) describe going through the paper’s first-person passages with Preece and revising them until he was satisfied. In the same article he recalls that communication partners sometimes softened the wording he used, including swearing. A polished account that preserves the general topic but removes a deliberately forceful expression preserves only part of the intended message. In interviews about AI writing assistance, Basoah et al. (2025) likewise documented differences between asking for help with an edit and inviting a system to imitate a particular register, including African American Vernacular English.

The useful editorial question is what this writer wanted to change on this occasion. If the volunteer wants the message shortened, the editor can do that without making the room available. A quick check after revision could ask the recipient to point to what they understood as confirmed and what they would still verify. This measures what the reader learned, not simply whether the edit made the original easier to skim. It checks a consequence that smooth reading, stylistic similarity, and the click that accepts a suggestion cannot settle.
