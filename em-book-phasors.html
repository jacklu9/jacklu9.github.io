Read Maxwell’s equations as simultaneous field constraints

The two divergence equations constrain net outward flux. The two curl equations constrain circulation and couple spatial field variation to time variation and current. They describe the same electric and magnetic fields at the same positions and times. It is useful to speak of induction, but the equations are not a sequence in which an electric field first creates a magnetic field from nothing, which then creates a new electric field. A physical solution must satisfy all four relations, the material model, the source specification, and appropriate initial and boundary conditions together. Nonzero fields can exist in a source-free region; ρ = 0 and J = 0 there do not mean that the fields or their curls vanish.

1.2.7 Time-harmonic fields and phasors

The changing field now has a place in the laws, but writing out its full time dependence repeatedly can obscure the comparison between readings. Change the drive to a sinusoidal source and consider the periodic response after its transient has decayed. What repeats from cycle to cycle, and what differs from point to point? Phasors will let us keep those pieces separate. The same idea also prepares us to read prescribed wave patterns; a single oscillating reading alone does not establish how a wave propagates.

One oscillation, three pieces of information. Imagine keeping a voltmeter at one location and watching its reading rise, fall, change sign, and repeat. Three numbers describe a sinusoidal reading: its largest magnitude, how long one repetition takes, and where in that repetition it begins. These are the peak amplitude A, the period T, and the initial phase ϕ. The amplitude has the units of the measured quantity; for a voltage it is in volts. The period is in seconds. Phase is an angle specifying a position within a cycle: adding 2π radians, or 360◦, gives the same reading.

Frequency f = 1/T counts cycles per second, in hertz (Hz). Angular frequency ω = 2πf = 2π/T counts radians per second. The accumulated phase at time t is therefore ωt + ϕ. A cosine begins at its positive maximum when this angle is zero:

For two signals of the same frequency, increasing the phase by ∆ϕ > 0 shifts a corresponding cycle feature earlier by ∆ϕ/ω. This is a phase lead; compare corresponding cycles because phase repeats modulo 2π. It is not a larger amplitude or a larger frequency.

A time-harmonic quantity has one sinusoidal frequency. In a linear, time-invariant system, a general excitation can be decomposed into Fourier components, solved frequency by frequency, and reconstructed by adding the responses. We first learn to handle one such component.

Why introduce a complex arrow? Draw an auxiliary plane with a horizontal real axis and a vertical imaginary axis. These axes are bookkeeping axes, not two directions in physical space. A point a + jb has horizontal coordinate a and vertical coordinate b, where j2 = −1. Multiplication by j rotates an arrow in this plane by 90◦ counterclockwise.

Euler’s identity says that an arrow of unit length at angle θ can be written as ejθ = cos θ + j sin θ. Here e is the base of the exponential function, and jθ is an imaginary exponent.
