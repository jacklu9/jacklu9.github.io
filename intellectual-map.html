Articles

Fifteen essays draw on published research in fields including design, learning, art, and computing. Each has its own sources, open question, and connections to other essays.

  1. Whose improvement counts?

    An assistive device can alter how an outing is organized, not merely how fast its user moves. Design tests should begin with the opportunity the person wants to gain.

  2. What a draft lets a person discover

    A sketch can reveal an unanticipated relation, and working timber can expose a requirement missing from the sketch. Comparing alternatives should make such discoveries easier to inspect.

  3. How a shared decision survives a handoff

    People adapt descriptions to a particular listener and repair misunderstandings as they work. A handoff needs to preserve decisions, their sources, and what remains open.

  4. The singer, the sensor, and the next sound

    A singer at a piano swayed and heard a soft noise change. When she tried switching registers, an operation central to her practice, the noise gave her no clear indication that she had done so.

  5. A finished worksheet is not the end of the lesson

    Two pupils hand in the same correct solution. One asked a chat tool what was wrong with the step she had already written.

  6. The choice a rewrite should leave open

    A poem can please its writer and still misdescribe the event that prompted it. Nicholes (2025) asked 88 university writers to compose a poem about a meaningful, difficult experience and to prompt a text generator to write about the same event.

  7. The evidence that could overturn an answer

    In an experiment with management consultants, the most convincing document did not always contain the best decision. The consultants had to recommend a brand from a spreadsheet and a set of interviews.

  8. A turn of one’s own

    During a cooking activity in a UK classroom, a teacher asked whether to add salt. A seven-year-old child looked repeatedly toward her speech-generating device.

  9. How many seconds a warning gives back

    In July 2019, two earthquakes near Ridgecrest, California, registered on the ShakeAlert network. The public app then serving Los Angeles County sent no notification for either event.

  10. A sentence in time to act

    A fact a person understands can still arrive too late to guide a choice, fail to support an action, or be mistaken for a promise.

  11. When a machine can be redirected

    Robot movements communicate intent, but someone who knows the intended action still needs a timely and meaningful way to change it.

  12. What a work allows someone to see

    A scaffold, gallery label, companion’s gesture, or film cut changes what a viewer can notice and show to another person.

  13. The work that only exists between teammates

    An essay on how collaborators carry decisions across changing designs, make dependencies visible, and get separately held evidence into a shared result.

  14. What the water queue leaves out

    Water access looks different when measured at a scheduled tap, during collection, and at the household. Field studies show what each measure omits.

  15. A simulated participant is not a recruit

    Simulations can suggest what to look for, reproduce selected responses, or rank interventions. Those uses place different demands on the evidence.

Open questions

Questions that grew out of the reading. Each leads back to the essay and its sources.

  1. Whose priorities define an improvement, and how can an evaluation capture gains the designer did not anticipate?
  2. When does comparing two representations change the problem someone thinks they are solving?
  3. How should a system preserve the status of a shared decision when the recipient or task changes?
  4. Which features of a measured signal can a performer intentionally change while leaving the rest alone?
  5. What should carry over from a supported task to a task where the support is no longer available?
  6. How can a tool make room for variation without treating every difference as an improvement?
  7. What observation would make a reasonable person actually revise their judgment?
  8. What would count as gaining a useful speaking turn rather than merely producing more words?
  9. How much of a forecast’s lead time survives the steps between prediction and action?
  10. What can a person do with a message by the time it reaches them?
  11. What does it take for a person to change an action after a machine has started it?
  12. How can an artwork’s presentation teach someone a way of looking without prescribing an interpretation?
  13. How can a team tell whether its separately finished work will serve the same purpose together?
  14. A queue got shorter. Did the same households obtain more reliable water, or did some stop arriving?
  15. What can a simulated answer justify before anyone recruits real participants?
